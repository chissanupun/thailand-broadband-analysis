\begin{abstract}
National Internet broadband service providers particularly in Thailand are
often perceived from user experiences. In Southeast Asian this assumption
remains largely unmeasured or studied. For example, Thailand ranked 12th
worldwide for fixed broadband in Ookla's Speedtest Global Index (June
2026)~\cite{OoklaGlobalIndex2026}, yet whether such the services reflect
application-level performance across the country is unclear. A 2023 survey
by Thailand Consumer Council found that 81\% of 2,924 respondents had
encountered connection problems, mostly slow and dropping
connections~\cite{TCC2023}. These numbers contradict with each other for
Thailand, and definitely, Thailand is not alone on the mismatched
information in Southeast Asian region.

In this study we analyze and evaluate the communication network quality of
nine countries: Thailand, Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar, Cambodia, Indonesia, the
Philippines, Malaysia, and Singapore using the test results from Q1 of
2023 to Q4 of 2025 using collected open datasets from Ookla (229.4 million
tests) and M-Lab NDT7 (760.6 million tests). We compared the results to the
bandwidth requirements of four types of popular application namely voice,
video streaming (HD), video streaming (UHD), and Cloud gaming.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
National Internet Service Providers (ISPs), particularly in Southeast Asian
countries like Thailand, are frequently judged based on subjective end-user
perceptions rather than systematic empirical data. A significant gap exists
between high-level speed index rankings and actual user experiences; for
example, while Thailand was ranked 12th worldwide for fixed broadband in
Ookla's Speedtest Global Index in June 2026, a 2023 survey by the Thailand
Consumer Council revealed that 81\% of 2,924 respondents suffered from
frequent connection problems such as slow speeds and dropped signals. To
address this widespread mismatch across the region, this paper presents a
comprehensive study analyzing communication network quality across nine
Southeast Asian nations: Thailand, Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar, Cambodia,
Indonesia, the Philippines, Malaysia, and Singapore. Leveraging
cross-platform open measurement datasets collected from Q1 2023 to Q4
2025---comprising 229.4 million tests from Ookla and 760.6 million tests
from Measurement Lab's NDT7---we evaluate performance against real-world
application demands.

\section{Data and Method}
Ookla open data supplies quarterly internet-performance tiles split by
connection type (fixed, mobile) at Quadkey zoom 16 ($\sim$600x600 m), each
carrying mean download, upload, latency, and the underlying Speedtest
submission count, delivered as global Parquet files on Amazon S3. We map
them to the nine countries and twelve quarters (Q1 2023-Q4 2025); having
the total of 229,367,221 tests, with largest Indonesia test data of 74.2
million records and smallest Laos dataset of 875,799 records. Total volume
across the nine countries from Mlab NDT7 is 760,607,387 tests, with
largest Indonesia test data of 367.2 million and smallest Laos test record
of 139,693 records.

The data from the two sources comes in different formats. Ookla comes as
spatial tiles approximately 600m x 600m, while NDT7 comes as individual
test records. We keep the two sources separate throughout and never pool
their records; we analyze each independently and compare the results side
by side. First we clean the data such as throughput $\leq$ 0 and duration
$\leq$ 0.
We then use ip-api.com~\cite{IpApi} which is a free API service for
checking whether the test records are the mobile / hosting / proxy flags
and the ASN for each IP. On Ookla dataset, we build a bounding box to
define each country's extent, referencing the geoBoundaries ADM1
file~\cite{Runfola2020} for the 9 ASEAN countries, then run
point-in-polygon on the tiles to determine which province each point falls
in. Then compute a weighted average by each tile's test count, to prevent
distortion from tiles with widely different test counts. To ensure
quality, we filter only province-quarters which has total\_tests $\geq$
100 and n\_tiles $\geq$ 5 which would better represent the reality.

\section{Data Analysis}
Across the nine Southeast Asian countries analyzed, Ookla fixed broadband
data (2023 Q1 -- 2025 Q4) reveals significant regional performance tiers
alongside pronounced urban-rural divides when comparing capital city
performance against national averages. Singapore leads the region overall,
with its Central Region reaching 361 Mbps, slightly below its national mean
(395 Mbps). In contrast, most other capital regions significantly
outperform their nationwide benchmarks: Bangkok Metropolis leads Thailand
at 299 Mbps (vs. 277 Mbps national), Kuala Lumpur drives Malaysia to
212 Mbps (vs. 195 Mbps national), Jakarta drives Indonesia to 63 Mbps (vs.
41 Mbps national), the Philippines' NCR reaches 160 Mbps (vs. 132 Mbps
national), and Hanoi records 161 Mbps for Vietnam (vs. 160 Mbps national).
At the lower end of the spectrum, Cambodia (Phnom Penh at 56 Mbps vs.
53 Mbps national) and Laos (Vientiane Capital at 56 Mbps vs. 51 Mbps
national) show modest capital premiums, while Myanmar (Yangon) trails at
22 Mbps, sitting slightly below its national average of 25 Mbps. Overall,
these metrics highlight a stark infrastructure gap where the fastest
capital (Singapore) reaches speeds 16.4 times greater than the slowest
capital (Yangon), with metropolitan centers generally serving as the
primary drivers of national connectivity.

For the national-level pass rate for voice and HD video
streaming---defined as the test-weighted share of province-quarters
achieving a download speed of $\geq$ 64Kbps and 5Mbps respectively, with
voice also requiring latency $\leq$ 200 ms---across nine Southeast Asian
countries from Q1 2024 through Q4 2025, nearly every country consistently
achieves a 100\% pass rate every quarter. The only departures are
Thailand's brief dip to 99.8\% in Q2 2024 and Myanmar's drop to 53.6\% in
Q2 2025, both driven by latency rather than bandwidth. This indicates that
across all evaluated nations, including lower-tier broadband markets,
existing fixed and mobile network infrastructure universally meets the
basic 5 Mbps threshold required for smooth HD video playback without
performance bottlenecks. However, the national pass rates for Ultra High
Definition (UHD) video streaming---defined as the test-weighted share of
province-quarters achieving download speeds of $\geq$ 25 Mbps---across the
same eight quarters reveal a clear divergence in performance. Malaysia,
Singapore, Thailand, the Philippines, Vietnam, Cambodia, and Laos
consistently achieve a 100\% pass rate throughout the entire period
(clustering together along the top boundary), with Indonesia briefly
dipping to 84.8\% in Q1 2024 before also settling at 100\%. Myanmar
consistently trails the region, with its UHD pass rate swinging between
10.5\% and 100\% over the two-year timeframe. Additionally, the national
pass rates for cloud gaming---defined by the test-weighted share of
province-quarters clearing both a bandwidth threshold of $\geq$ 44 Mbps and
a latency threshold of $\leq$ 25 ms---across nine Southeast Asian nations
from Q1 2024 to Q4 2025 show Singapore and Vietnam forming the top tier,
both maintaining a near-perfect 100\% pass rate throughout, while Thailand
and Malaysia fluctuate slightly around 96\%--100\%. The Philippines and
Cambodia occupy a mid-tier position, ranging between 87\% and 99\%, while
Laos fluctuates more widely between 67\% and 100\%. Indonesia shows a
marked recovery, climbing from 24.5\% in Q1 2024 to 91.0\% by Q4 2025.
Myanmar fails to clear the combined threshold in any quarter, holding at
0\% throughout: every province-quarter that clears the 44 Mbps bandwidth
bar still falls short of the 25 ms
latency requirement.

\section{Conclusion}
This study provides a multi-year empirical evaluation of network
infrastructure performance and application readiness across nine
Southeast Asian nations using over 990 million tests from Ookla and M-Lab
NDT7 datasets. Our findings reveal that while foundational network
requirements---such as voice calls and HD video streaming $\geq$ 5
Mbps---are met with a pass rate at or near 100\% across nearly all
evaluated countries, significant digital divides emerge under higher-tier
demands.
Fixed broadband infrastructure exhibits stark regional stratification and
severe urban-rural disparities, with the fastest capital reaching speeds
up to 16.4 times higher than the slowest capital. While mobile
networks provide a more spatially uniform and reliable baseline in
developing economies like Myanmar, Laos, and Cambodia, they struggle to
sustain bandwidth-intensive, latency-critical workloads. Ultimately, this
research underscores that raw speed rankings fail to reflect end-to-end
user experiences across diverse application types, highlighting the
imperative for targeted fiber deployment, regional infrastructure
investments, and transport-layer optimizations to achieve true digital
equity across Southeast Asia. The province-quarterly tables also support
peak-hour, per-operator, and representativeness analyses left for future
work.
