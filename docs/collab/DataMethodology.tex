\section{Data and Methodology}

\subsection{Datasets}

\textbf{Ookla Open Data} supplies quarterly internet-performance tiles split by connection
type (fixed, mobile) at Quadkey zoom 16 ($\sim$610$\times$610~m), each carrying mean
download, upload, latency, and the underlying Speedtest submission count, delivered as
global Parquet files on Amazon S3 that we filter down to our eight countries ourselves.
Across twelve quarters (Q1~2023--Q4~2025) and both products this totals 224{,}024{,}732
tests, with Indonesia largest at 74.2~million and Laos smallest at 875{,}799.

\textbf{M-Lab NDT7} supplies per-test records over the same range via BigQuery, retaining
per-test timestamps and raw ISP attribution that Ookla's tiles discard --- what makes RQ3
and RQ4 possible at all. Total volume across the eight countries is 723{,}938{,}064 tests,
with Indonesia largest at 367.2~million and Laos smallest at 139{,}693. NDT7 does not
distinguish fixed from mobile natively: client IPs are grouped into /24 (IPv4) or /48
(IPv6) blocks, five IPs per block are queried against ip-api's \texttt{mobile} flag, and the
majority result is applied to the whole block; when the five samples disagree, every IP in
that block is checked individually. This sample-and-escalate design cuts total query time
from 27~days to a few hours. Operators are grouped by \textbf{ASN} rather than by M-Lab's
raw ISP-name field, which is unreliable at scale. Validated against 5.24~million
individually-labelled IPs from earlier fetch rounds (84{,}137 blocks), the method
reproduces ip-api's own per-IP answer at least 99.15\% of the time, with truly mixed blocks
estimated at only 0.45\% --- though a unanimous 5-of-5 sample is only a lower bound on a
block's homogeneity, not proof of it, since the check has no ground truth independent of
ip-api itself.

\textbf{Boundaries and reference data.} Country-level bounding boxes come from geoBoundaries
ADM1; province assignment uses GADM~4.1 point-in-polygon, except Thailand, which uses the
project's own 77-province geojson because GADM's administrative levels there were not
granular enough. Each province-quarter row also carries population, density, GDP per
capita, and an internet-infrastructure tier from a project reference table.

Coverage is not uniform even before the reliability gate. Vietnam and Cambodia are missing
mobile-side data for some provinces in some quarters, reflecting small geographic areas
with low test volume; Myanmar's gap is substantial, missing on both fixed (10.7\%) and
mobile (22\%), concentrated mostly after 2024. None of this is an artefact of our
filtering --- it was never collected in the first place.

\subsection{Aggregation and filtering}

Ookla tiles are assigned to provinces by point-in-polygon against each country's geojson
and aggregated to province-quarter as a weighted average of each tile's mean value by its
own test count. The weighting is not optional: tile submission counts differ by orders of
magnitude, and an unweighted mean would let a sparsely-tested tile distort a province's
figure as much as a heavily-tested one. NDT7 provinces are assigned the same way against
GADM~4.1 boundaries, aggregating directly from per-test records.

Every province-quarter is filtered on measurement sufficiency before any statistic is
computed: \texttt{total\_tests} $\geq$ 100 \textbf{AND} \texttt{n\_tiles} $\geq$ 5 for
Ookla, \texttt{total\_tests} $\geq$ 100 alone for NDT7. The criterion removes nothing that
was not already missing --- on the Ookla side it excludes no rows at all: across the eight
countries, 3{,}162 fixed province-quarters enter and 3{,}162 pass, and 3{,}124 mobile
province-quarters enter and 3{,}124 pass. Its effect on NDT7 is not uniform: cellular test
counts drop in every country under this gate, most severely in Vietnam, and Cambodia and
Laos --- already the two lowest-volume countries in the study --- lose a large share of
both fixed and mobile data. It is a real constraint on NDT7 and a formality on Ookla.

Raw records are cleaned before aggregation: implausible rows (\texttt{throughput} $\leq$ 0,
\texttt{duration} $\leq$ 0, and similar) are filtered out ahead of any statistic being
computed, on both sources.
